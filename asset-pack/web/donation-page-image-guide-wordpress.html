For every image

  • JPG for photos and illustrations, PNG for logos.
  • Send the original files, not screenshots or images saved from social media.
  • No words, prices or dates in the image. We add your headline to the page as text. The one exception is Banner style, explained below.
  • Only use photos you have the rights to, with consent from anyone who can be recognised.
Your logo

A small, transparent logo that reads on dark

Your logo appears at the top of every donation page, usually on top of your image.

Shape3:1

Max file200 KB

Why we ask for a light version

On most layouts your logo sits over your image, which has a soft dark overlay so white text stays readable. That overlay is there even when your photo is light, so a dark logo would disappear against it. Please send a version with white or light text.

  • A symbol plus your name reads best at this small size
  • Transparent background, with no white box around it
  • If you can, export at 3× (720 × 240) for extra-sharp screens

Left: light version, used on your page. Right: dark version, used on white backgrounds.

Only have a dark logo, or a light image?

  • Classic or Compact layout: choose Banner style. Your image is shown whole and untouched, with no overlay, and your headline goes below it. Your artwork can then carry your dark logo itself.
  • Any other layout: the overlay is always on, so a light logo is needed. If you only have a dark version, send it and we can create a white version for you.
Layout guides

Each layout, in detail

For each layout: what to supply, how it is cropped on a computer and a phone, and the finished page for our two example charities.

Layout guide

Classic

Best for: Bigger gifts and flagship appeals

Image size1600 × 600 px
Shape8:3 wide
FormatJPG or PNG
Max file2 MB

How it sits on the page

A wide banner across the top of a step-by-step donation form. The top and bottom edges are trimmed, and trimmed more on a phone, where the banner becomes almost square.

Where to put the subject

Keep the main subject in the middle of the frame. Treat the top and bottom 15% or so as edges that may be cut off.

What covers the image

Your logo, charity name, headline and description sit over the left and middle of the image. A soft dark overlay keeps the white text readable.

Do

  • A calm scene with one clear focal point in the centre
  • Simple sky or background where the headline sits
  • A subject that is still recognisable when cropped to a square

Avoid

  • Faces or detail near the top or bottom edge
  • Words, logos or dates in the image (unless you use Banner style)
  • Very busy scenes, which fight the headline

Prefer your artwork untouched? Use Banner style

On this layout we can show your image whole, with no cropping, no darkening and nothing placed over it. Your headline then appears below the image. Use it for designed artwork that already includes your logo and title, or if your image is light and your logo is dark.

Keep any words in the artwork large, because a phone shows it at about a quarter of the size. Tell us what the artwork says, so we can add it for screen-reader users.

Shape5:2 wide
FormatJPG or PNG
Max file1.5 MB

How it sits on the page

A compact banner above a narrow, single-page donation form. It looks the same size on a computer and on a phone.

Where to put the subject

Centre the subject and keep it in the upper half. The lower-left quarter must stay clear.

What covers the image

Your logo sits top-left. The headline sits across the lower-left, which is why that corner needs to stay quiet.

Do

  • A single object or close-up (a bouquet, a meal, a hand)
  • Plain or softly textured lower-left corner
  • Strong colour that still reads at a small size

Avoid

  • Anything important in the lower-left
  • Wide landscapes with small detail, which disappear at this size
  • Re-using a Classic 1600 × 600 image, which crops badly

Hero banner

Best for: Events and fundraising goals

Image size1920 × 600 px
Shape16:5 extra-wide
FormatJPG or PNG
Max file2.5 MB

How it sits on the page

A thin strip across the full width of the page. Only a narrow band through the middle of the image is visible on a computer screen.

Where to put the subject

Run your subject across the horizontal middle. Treat the top and bottom thirds as background that will mostly be hidden.

What covers the image

Your logo, charity name, headline and description sit in one line across the strip.

Do

  • A repeating or panoramic subject (a crowd, a skyline, a flower border)
  • Detail concentrated in the middle band
  • Even tone across the width, so text reads everywhere

Avoid

  • A single subject in the centre, which ends up behind the headline
  • Any detail near the top or bottom
  • Busy high-contrast patterns behind the text

Tips

Nine tips for a page that inspires giving

Use these alongside the guidance for your layout.

1One subject, one feeling

Choose a photo with one clear focal point and an emotion that matches your appeal: hope, warmth, dignity. Busy collages fight the headline.

2Think about the crop

Check the crop diagram for your layout. The same image looks very different on a computer and a phone, so keep the important part inside both outlines.

3Leave room for the words

Your headline sits on the image. Photos with sky, a wall or a soft background where the text goes work best.

4Mid to dark tones

White text sits on your image under a soft dark overlay. Very pale or very busy photos make text harder to read. We can adjust the overlay if needed.

5Real people, with consent

Your own people and places beat stock photos. Get written consent from anyone recognisable, and take extra care with children and patients.

6Sensitive causes

For hospice, bereavement or health causes, show hope rather than illness: gardens, light, hands, family moments. Flowers and nature suit in-memory giving.

7Colours from your brand

Send your brand colours as hex codes, such as #4B2E4F. We use your main colour for links and highlights and your accent colour for the donate button.

8Export at full size

Send images at the size listed for your layout. They are already double-size so they stay sharp on modern screens. JPG at high quality is ideal.

9Send the files themselves

Attach the files or send a download link. If you replace an image later, give it a new filename so the update shows straight away.

Before you send: a 30-second check

  • The image is the right size for your layout
  • The main subject sits inside both crop outlines
  • No words, logos or dates in the image (unless you chose Banner style)
  • You have consent from anyone who can be recognised
  • Your logo has a transparent background and light text
  • Each file is under the size limit

What happens next

We build your page and send you a private preview link to check it on your computer and phone. Once you're happy, we make it live.
